HOW IT WORKS

What actually counts as one commit.

No hidden formulas. Here's exactly what the extension watches for, and why each rule exists.

01

A full watch, not a glance

Instagram Reels loop. A commit is only logged when the video's playhead wraps from near the end back to the start — or fires a natural "ended" event. Skips, previews, and half-scrolls never register.

02

Only what's actually on screen

Instagram preloads upcoming Reels before you scroll to them. An IntersectionObserver checks that the video is genuinely visible before any commit counts.

03

Your local calendar day, not UTC

Each commit is filed under your device's local date, so a late-night scroll lands on the day you actually experienced it.

04

Three zones, one 15-minute budget

15 minutes a day, at roughly 30 seconds per Reel, works out to about 30 commits. 0–5 is green, 6–30 is yellow, 31+ is red.

05

Streaks forgive gaps, not relapses

A day with zero recorded data is treated as a gap, not a break. A single red day is the only thing that resets your streak to zero.

Notes from actually shipping this.

Build notes, not announcements — what broke, what got cut, and what's next.

🟩
v0.1Detection

Why "progress ≥ 98%" doesn't work on a looping Reel

The first naive completion check either never fired or fired constantly. The fix was detecting the loop-wrap itself.

🟨
v0.1Storage

UTC dates put my commits on the wrong day

toISOString() is UTC. Every date key now comes from local getFullYear/getMonth/getDate.

🟥
v0.2Streaks

A missing day isn't the same as a red day

The first streak logic reset to zero on install. Now a day with no recorded data is a gap, not a break.

ABOUT

I built this because my own hand kept doing it.

A solo, final-year engineering project. This is who's behind it, why it exists, and what it will never do.

The story

During my diploma years, my phone access was restricted. Then B.Tech happened, and with it, PDFs replaced textbooks — a phone stopped being optional and became the thing I had to open just to study. That shift, from restriction to constant access, is where the habit started.

I already track real progress on GitHub, and watching that grid fill in over a year is genuinely satisfying. I wanted the same clear, undeniable visual for the time I was losing — not a lecture, not a blocker that gets bypassed, just the truth laid out one square at a time.

What APCG does

  • Counts a Reel as "watched" only when it's actually watched to completion;
  • Turns that count into a private, GitHub-style grid — one square per day;
  • Keeps every byte of that data on your own device, by default, forever;
  • Tracks streaks and a small, non-monetary badge — nothing you can buy your way into.

Why this exists

Screen-time tools usually hide the data behind a settings menu, or block access outright and get bypassed within a day. APCG doesn't block anything — it just makes the invisible visible, the same way a GitHub streak does, except inverted. An empty grid is the win.

What APCG will never do

  • It will never make your grid public or visible to anyone but you.
  • It will never upload your data anywhere without you explicitly turning on sync.
  • It will never gate features behind a subscription or a "streak freeze" purchase.
  • It will never require an account just to see your own numbers.

Roadmap

  • Harden Reel-completion detection against Instagram's real, changing DOM;
  • A red-zone content filter that gently blurs the Reels tab past the daily budget;
  • An opt-in, login-free sync code for viewing one grid across two devices;
  • An honest mobile path (Firefox for Android) instead of promising native-app support that isn't possible.

PRIVACY

Your data never leaves your device.

No account, no server, no analytics SDK. Here's exactly what that means.

What's stored

One number per calendar day: how many Reels you fully watched. That's it. It lives in your browser's local extension storage (chrome.storage.local).

What's never collected

  • No video content, thumbnails, or captions are read or stored;
  • No account, email, or login is ever required;
  • No usage data is sent to any server — there is currently no server;
  • No data is shared with Instagram, Meta, or any third party.

If sync is ever added

A future, fully optional sync feature would use a random, locally-generated code — never an email or password. Turning it on would always be an explicit choice, never a default.

Uninstalling

Removing the extension deletes its local storage along with it. There's nothing left behind to "delete your account" for, because there was never an account.
